# Solar Home Power System — User and Maintenance Manual

## 1. Introducing the Solar Home series

The Solar Home series is a complete off-grid power package designed for homes that want predictable electricity without depending on the public grid. Every kit ships as a single crate containing the inverter, the battery pack, the roof panels, the wiring harness, the mounting brackets, and a printed quick-start card that guides you through the first connection in under an hour. The philosophy behind the series is straightforward: store cheap daytime solar energy in a battery during the day, then draw that stored energy in the evening and during outages, so that your lights, fridge, router, and a few essential appliances keep running even when the grid is down. All kits share the same inverter platform and the same mobile application, which reports live production, live consumption, charge level, and estimated hours of backup remaining on a single screen, so the differences between the kits are really only about how much roof area you have, how much energy your household uses, and how long you want to be able to run without sunlight.

## 2. Kit lineup, specifications, and pricing

The series currently spans three kits, and the physical hardware is nearly identical across all of them, with the differences concentrated in the panel count, the inverter capacity, the battery size, and therefore the price tag. The entry model is the Solar Home Mini, which carries the model number SHM-400, a four hundred watt panel array, a five kilowatt-hour battery, and is aimed at apartments and small two-room households that only need to power lighting, a phone charger, a laptop, and a small refrigerator for a few hours in the evening. The mid-range kit is the Solar Home Plus, model SHM-800, which steps up to an eight hundred watt array and a ten kilowatt-hour battery and is the recommended starting point for a medium family home because it can carry the router, the television, the ceiling fans, and the refrigerator through the whole night on a single charge. The flagship kit is the Solar Home Max, model SHM-1200, which offers a twelve hundred watt array with a fifteen kilowatt-hour battery and is intended for larger houses where the user also wants to run a washing machine, a water pump, or a small air conditioner during the evening load. ## 3. Installation, siting, and the coastal note

Installation should always be performed by a certified installer because the roof mounting brackets have to carry the full wind load of the panels in your location, and a bracket that is undersized for the local wind speed can rip the entire array off the roof during a storm. The panels should face true south in the northern hemisphere, or true north in the southern hemisphere, and should be tilted to match your latitude plus roughly ten degrees, because that tilt can change annual production by as much as fifteen percent. The battery pack must be installed indoors, in a dry and ventilated space, at least fifty centimeters away from walls and away from any direct sunlight or heat source, because the cells discharge best between fifteen and thirty degrees Celsius and will age faster when they sit hot. If the house is located within about five kilometers of the coast, the installer must additionally apply the corrosion protection kit, which consists of a marine-grade coating for all exposed metal fittings and a sealed junction box for the outdoor wiring, because salt-laden air attacks untreated connectors and can reduce the life of the outdoor equipment by several years. The same coastal note applies to the charge controller fins, which should be cleaned more often in coastal use because salt dust settles on the heat sinks and insulates them, and this cleaning habit is listed again under maintenance further down, but it is worth repeating here because it is the single most common reason for premature controller failure in seaside homes.

## 4. Battery capacity and expected runtime

The battery is the heart of the system, and understanding its capacity is the key to knowing what the system can actually do for you. Battery capacity is measured in kilowatt-hours, and the number on the specification sheet is the total energy the battery can hold when it is fully charged. The usable energy however is always a little lower than the nameplate value, because the inverter stops drawing when the battery reaches about twenty percent charge to protect the cells, so a five kilowatt-hour battery gives you roughly four usable kilowatt-hours in practice, and a ten kilowatt-hour battery gives you roughly eight. To estimate runtime, divide the usable energy by the power your home is actually drawing at that moment, because the battery drains faster when the load is heavy, which is exactly the same relationship that governs how long a water tank lasts when you open the tap wider. For example, at a steady draw of one thousand watts, a fully charged ten kilowatt-hour battery delivers about eight hours of backup, whereas the same battery at a modest five hundred watt draw lasts about sixteen hours, and the mobile application performs this exact calculation live and shows the remaining hours on the main screen so you never have to do the arithmetic yourself.

Battery aging is governed more by how deep the discharge is than by how old the battery is, because every complete drain damages the cells a little and many shallow drains are much kinder than few deep ones. The charging profile built into the inverter therefore stops charging at one hundred percent and starts supplying the home directly whenever the battery has crossed the eighty percent threshold during the day, because holding the battery at a high state of charge near the very top of its range also accelerates aging, and keeping it between twenty and eighty percent for the majority of its life extends the usable cycles dramatically. Under this balanced regime the manufacturer rates the pack for ten years of service or six thousand full discharge cycles, whichever comes first, and most owners in ordinary use report that the pack still holds at least ninety percent of its original capacity at the five year mark. The warranty itself, and the exact claiming process, is covered in the next section, and the runtime and protection figures above are the numbers you should quote when a customer asks how long the battery will genuinely last on a real evening.

## 5. Maintenance habits and warranty coverage

Routine maintenance is deliberately light because the inverter monitors everything, but a handful of habits keep the system at full health for the entire warranty period and beyond. The panels should be rinsed with plain water every couple of months in dusty areas, because a layer of dust or bird droppings can cut production by ten percent or more, while the controller heat sinks and the battery vents should be vacuumed or wiped at the same time, and the coastal cleaning note from the installation section applies here with double force for houses near the sea. The mobile application logs production and battery health continuously, and the thing to watch is the daily production trend, because a slow and steady drop in production, rather than a sudden collapse, is normally the first sign that the panels need a rinse or that a connector is slowly oxidizing. Every six months the installer should re-torque the roof bolts and inspect the mounting brackets for corrosion, because hardware that looks fine in a photograph can still be working loose underneath, and this inspection is the cheapest insurance you will ever buy for a roof-mounted installation.

The kit warranty from the table covers the panels, the inverter, and the wiring for the advertised period, which starts on the install date and covers parts and labor for any manufacturing defect. The battery packs are covered separately, for ten years from the install date or six thousand cycles, whichever comes first, and this battery warranty is the reason the mobile application records charge cycles automatically, because an accurate cycle count is the evidence used to settle any claim. If the inverter reports a fault during the warranty period, you simply open a claim through the application, upload the diagnostic summary it generates, and the support team ships a replacement or schedules a repair visit within seven days in most regions, while out-of-warranty units are serviced at a flat hourly rate with the parts billed at list price. The honest expectation, however, is that you will rarely need the process, because the design goal of the series is a system that quietly does its job for a decade, and the maintenance habits above are exactly what the engineers themselves follow in their own homes.
